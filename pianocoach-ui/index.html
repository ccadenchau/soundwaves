The pianist’s audio & musicality guide

Use measured results as clues, then listen to the recording. Attack velocity is an estimate; tone color, technique, and pedal choices still need your ear.

1 · Tone & touch

Let the melody sing with a clear, supported sound. Choose pearly, warm, or percussive color to fit the music. Use comfortable arm weight rather than a sharp strike for fortissimo; find the soft pianissimo that still speaks.

2 · Voicing & texture

Bring the lead line forward. Let the bass anchor it, keep inner voices transparent, and make accompaniment a soft cushion. Check that chords do not drown out the melody.

3 · Phrasing & agogic accent

Shape each phrase like a sentence: rise toward its peak, then release. Use flexible timing while keeping the pulse present. Give important notes a little extra time when the phrase asks for it instead of making every accent louder.

4 · Articulation as speech

Make staccato crisp or warmly detached, legato connected, and portato gently weighted. Let a resolving note taper and release slightly early rather than punching it. Compare note lengths with the score, allowing for pedal and transcription uncertainty.

5 · Acoustic awareness

Listen past each attack to the decay and how it blends with the next harmony. Clear the sustain pedal when harmony changes. Record a take and listen back for balance, resonance, and phrase shape.
